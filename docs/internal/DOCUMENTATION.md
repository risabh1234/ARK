# Documentation — Product, Brand & Methodology Reference

Internal. This is the non-technical counterpart to TECHNICAL_DOCUMENTATION.md — it exists
because the original spec (`~/Desktop/Design.pdf`, "Aroha — Design & Go-to-Market
Specification") isn't itself in this repo. Anyone who needs to write new copy, add a page, or
make a judgment call about tone should read this first rather than re-deriving it from the live
site. If the two ever disagree, treat this file as the record of *intent* and the site as the
record of *current state* — reconcile rather than assuming one is wrong.

## What Aroha is

A knowledge-instrument company, explicitly **not** a spiritual brand — that distinction is
load-bearing and shows up everywhere in the voice rules below. Positioning line: *"Aroha builds
research and intelligence tools for people who would rather understand something completely than
believe it quickly."*

One-sentence thesis: **people are not uninformed, they are unintegrated** — a modern person
carries four incompatible accounts of the world (scientific/material, market/ambition,
therapeutic/desire, inherited/meaning) with no shared vocabulary between them. Aroha's stated job
is to give that dissonance a name and then a structure — "one architecture in which knowledge,
ambition, desire and meaning all have a correct place."

The interpretive framework itself (the thing that decides what counts as evidence, how sources
rank, how conclusions are reached) is **never named or surfaced directly** — it's demonstrated
through the method, not marketed as a belief. "Gravity is not marketed as a belief. It is
described, demonstrated, and used." Treat this the way you'd treat a company's actual algorithm:
real, consistent, referred to obliquely, never pitched.

## Revenue path

`Studio (cash now) → Briefs (volume) → Codex (platform, later)`. Studio commissions fund the
company today; briefs build the catalogue and audience; Codex is the eventual product this is all
in service of. When making a product-priority call, that's the order of what actually pays rent.

## Voice — what never appears on the surface

Hard "never" list, regardless of how well something might convert:

- Saffron, marigold, lotus, om, tilak, deity imagery, mandala geometry — no devotional visual
  vocabulary, ever.
- Sanskrit in any H1, nav item, button, or product name.
- "Vedic AI", "ancient wisdom", "spiritual technology", "sacred" — or any phrase in that register.
- Devotional adjectives: divine, blessed, timeless, eternal.
- Any sentence that assumes the reader already agrees.
- Moral instruction about behaviour, dress, diet or discipline.

What's load-bearing underneath instead (present in the actual research/interpretive practice,
just never announced as such):

- A stated, examinable source hierarchy — what outranks what, and why.
- Strict separation between what a source says and how Aroha reads/interprets it.
- Declared uncertainty — every brief has an Unresolved section.
- A four-fold treatment of human aims, presented as psychology, not doctrine.
- Classical logic as the analytic spine, taught as reasoning, not religion.
- Specialist terminology only appears inside a brief, defined on first use — never as
  decoration on the surface.

**The test for every sentence published anywhere on the site:** *could a curious atheist read
this and nod?* If not, rewrite it. The framework is the foundation, not the pitch.

## The habit loop (why the site is built the way it is)

Stated model: a habit forms when an internal itch reliably finds a fast action, an uncertain
reward, and something the user leaves behind. Aroha's internal trigger is framed as **unresolved
curiosity** — the feeling of having half an answer. Every surface is meant to either create that
feeling or resolve it; nothing is meant to be neutral filler.

Six priming devices this implies (reference for anyone adding a new page or feature):

1. **The open loop** — every brief shows its own Unresolved list, visible before purchase.
2. **The counter** — real labour numbers ("sources read: 63"), never engagement/follower counts.
3. **The single-question landing** — one question, one paragraph, one field, nothing else.
4. **Progressive disclosure** — free tier gets the argument; paid gets sources, counter-
   arguments, working notes (this is what `DepthControl`'s Quick/Explain vs Deep/Complete models).
5. **The scarcity that's true** — Studio's "three commissions a month" is a stated operational
   fact, not manufactured urgency.
6. **Depth control** — Quick / Explain / Deep / Complete on every published piece; the reader
   chooses exposure. Doubles as a live preview of the future Codex product.

Hard limits paired with that (also the substance of the `/docs` "what we deliberately don't do"
section — keep that section's claims true against this list if either changes):

- Never a fake scarcity timer.
- No infinite scroll, no notification badges.
- No exit popups, no chat bubbles, no cookie theatre.
- One CTA per screen — two choices is hesitation, not choice.
- Loading is a moment, not a spinner — reveal once, then leave the reader alone.

001 is the deliberately-chosen first brief: universal (everyone has wanted something badly and
been unmoved on arrival), unclaimed by any existing genre (self-help moralises about it,
neuroscience reduces it, nobody architects it), and the exact place where the framework is
strongest and least detectable — desire treated as one of four legitimate human aims, arguable on
psychology/economics/evidence alone, no religious language required.

## Studio

Three tiers, prices published on the page on purpose ("it filters out everyone who was never
going to pay"): Sprint ₹25,000 (one question, 5 working days), Dossier ₹75,000 (full research
architecture, 3 weeks), Retainer ₹1,50,000/month (embedded research desk). Handover on every
engagement: research memo, annotated source ledger, timeline, counterargument file, terminology
sheet, a 30-minute call. Rule of thumb from the spec: ship with one excellent paid brief, not six
thin ones — "a shop with one excellent item reads as a press, a shop with six thin items reads as
a content farm."

## Vision / Codex

Public framing for Aroha Codex ("we are building an instrument, and this is the beginning of
it") should read like a hardware company introducing a machine still in the lab, not a mission
statement. Approved language: *"a library that can think with you," "every answer carries its
sources, or it is not an answer," "it works with the network off."* Banned language:
"AI-powered," "revolutionary," "the future of," "spiritual," any Sanskrit product name.

## Funnel (for anyone reasoning about growth work)

`Reach (one dangerous question, ~30s) → Land (single-question page, one field) → Give (the
Primer, instant, in-browser) → Earn (five letters, no pitch until the fifth) → Sell (Brief 001) →
Keep (weekly letter, saved questions) → Compound (Codex cohort waitlist)`. Spec's own month-one
revenue model, for reference: 2 Studio sprints (₹50,000) + 1 Dossier (₹75,000) + 40 briefs
(₹51,960) ≈ ₹1,76,960, with the explicit note that 3 direct-approach clients out-earn 40
algorithm-found customers and arrive faster.
