# References — cross-domain audit

**Status: FILLED — Phase 0 deliverable. 15 entries; 12 non-web (80%).**
Format per entry: *what it is → what exactly to steal → what it teaches about ĀRK's problem.*
Web entries are deliberately few and chosen as proof-of-pattern, not mood boards.

## Non-web (primary mining ground)

1. **Dieter Rams' Braun control panels (ET66 calculator, T3/T1000 radios)** — the canon of
   instrument-honest design.
   → Steal: every control physically states its effect; labels set in one voice; a single accent
   (the yellow/green power keys) rationed to the function that matters most.
   → Teaches: an interface can be dense and calm at once if each element declares its job —
   direct precedent for rendering DepthControl and CTA ration.

2. **Tektronix 7000-series analog oscilloscopes — graticule + phosphor CRT.**
   → Steal: measurement grid integrated *into* the display surface (engraved divisions, not
   decoration); glow is emitted by the signal, never by the bezel.
   → Teaches: furniture can measure. A background device on the landing could structure content
   and carry meaning simultaneously — the antidote to `.hero-glow`.

3. **Wild Heerbrugg / Zeiss laboratory microscopes.**
   → Steal: engraved scales, knurled focus discs, stage plates — precision made tactile;
   magnification changes feel mechanical, not magical.
   → Teaches: DepthControl-style toggles should feel like turning a turret, not tapping a pill —
   affordance language for "reader chooses exposure".

4. **Aircraft Quick Reference Handbooks (Airbus/Boeing checklists).**
   → Steal: challenge–response two-column typography; abnormal items visually promoted, not
   hidden; zero ornament under maximum stakes.
   → Teaches: the strata table's ideal typographic shape — labelled pairs where the label is
   load-bearing, used at the exact moment trust must be earned.

5. **Oxford Classical Texts / Teubner critical editions — apparatus criticus.**
   → Steal: two text densities on one page (main reading text; small-caps sigla apparatus below),
   with a legend that makes the small layer decodable.
   → Teaches: provenance can live permanently in margins without destroying reading flow — the
   strongest printed precedent for a Provenance Rail / Apparatus Line.

6. **Library card catalogues and archival finding aids.**
   → Steal: drawer labels, printed guide cards, call numbers — navigation by metadata, authority
   communicated by system, not by styling.
   → Teaches: a research catalogue can present five briefs with dignity and no thumbnails;
   BriefRow's instinct was right and can go further.

7. **Natural-history museum specimen labels and vitrines (NHM/London, MNHN/Paris).**
   → Steal: the label hierarchy — locality, date, collector, determination, revision ("det.
   1898; rev. 1974") — uncertainty recorded as part of the artefact's identity.
   → Teaches: how to typeset source metadata so honesty reads as prestige rather than
   housekeeping.

8. **Geological survey maps (USGS, British Geological Survey) with stratigraphic columns.**
   → Steal: the legend as claim taxonomy — you cannot read the map without the column beside it;
   strata drawn in canonical order, oldest to youngest.
   → Teaches: the Strata Ruler seed — page sections mapped to evidence layers I–IV with a always-
   visible key; also the honest answer to "what makes our anatomy table more than a table".

9. **Otto Neurath's ISOTYPE charts.**
   → Steal: quantity expressed by repeated standardized glyphs; no perspective, no gradient, no
   3D; the picture *is* the count.
   → Teaches: how to render "63 sources" or "three commissions" as pictures of labour — counters
   as instrumentation, immune to vanity-metric smell.

10. **Survey of India sheets / Great Trigonometrical Survey engraving culture.**
    → Steal: graticule marginalia, bench-mark stamps, the sheet edge carrying the survey's own
    metadata; precision as national-technical heritage, entirely secular.
    → Teaches: an Indian register of rigour exists outside devotional art — usable under the
    voice law; marginalia-as-truth over decoration-as-beauty.

11. **Edward Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information* / *Envisioning Information*.**
    → Steal: chartjunk doctrine (data-ink ratio), small multiples, annotation replacing legend
    where possible, footnotes set tight and quiet.
    → Teaches: candidate constitution law material — "every mark either carries data or is
    removed" translates directly to UI.

12. **Harry Beck's London tube diagram (1933).**
    → Steal: abstraction under stated rules (only horizontal/vertical/45°); geography sacrificed
    to topology because the argument required it.
    → Teaches: diagram-native imagery done right — simplify until the structure is geometric;
    what BrokenMapDiagram should have been.

## Web (proof-of-pattern, ≤40%)

13. **Wikipedia citation apparatus (ref tooltips, [citation needed] badges).**
    → Steal: provenance revealed in-flow without leaving the reading surface; absence of sources
    marked visibly, not hidden.
    → Teaches: progressive disclosure of sources at planetary scale; Unresolved rendered as UI,
    which is ĀRK's differentiator already invented — with better typography it becomes identity.

14. **arXiv abstract pages.**
    → Steal: ID-first header, submission-history transparency, grey-box austerity that signals
    institutional trust; zero marketing chrome.
    → Teaches: anti-SaaS layout proof — administrative transparency itself reads as credibility;
    supports burying marketing furniture and elevating record-like surfaces.

15. **NASA Eyes / JPL live mission telemetry pages.**
    → Steal: live true numbers as spectacle — dials whose motion *is* the information.
    → Teaches: the Instrument Panel hero concept's proof case; motion budget spent on one
    meaningful live element beats six decorative animations.

## Phase 1 additions (researched for the Atlas sibling pool)

16. **Kevin Lynch, *The Image of the City* (MIT Press, 1960)** — field studies of Boston/Jersey
    City/LA; residents' mental maps reduce to five elements (paths, edges, districts, nodes,
    landmarks); coined "imageability" and "wayfinding".
    → Steal: imageability as an acceptance test ("sketch the page from memory"); districts and
    few-strong-landmarks as page structure; wayfinding without signage.
    → Teaches: memorability is infrastructure, not decoration; a page that can be redrawn from
    memory has installed understanding without asserting it.

17. **J.B. Harley, "Deconstructing the Map" (*Cartographica* 26:2, 1989); Denis Wood, *The Power
    of Maps* (Guilford, 1992)** — critical cartography: maps assert rather than mirror; map
    authority historically flowed from *erased* authorship; margins/verso carry a second text;
    a map links territory to what comes with it.
    → Steal: the inversion ĀRK can own — show authorship instead of erasing it (the framework is
    stated, examinable); margins as first-class text.
    → Teaches: the theoretical spine of L2/L4 — provenance furniture is not garnish; it is the
    difference between a window-dressed-as-truth and an argument that owns itself.

18. **NOAA *U.S. Chart No. 1* + Coast Survey source-diagram practice** — the published symbol
    standard; "doubtful sounding", unsounded-area and swept-channel markings; large-scale charts
    print a source diagram showing which decade surveyed which water.
    → Steal: doubt rendered typographically (bracketed figures!); survey-vintage printed beside
    data; blanks declared with boundaries, not hidden.
    → Teaches: uncertainty has a 200-year-old visual grammar ready to borrow; a legend can be a
    published standard readers learn once and trust everywhere.

19. **ICAO Doc 8168 (PANS-OPS) & FAA Order 8260.3 approach-chart construction** — plan view +
    profile view + tabular minima on the verso; missed-approach procedure mandatory before it is
    needed; minimum boxes vary by aircraft category, openly.
    → Steal: three-register presentation of one procedure (structure/descent/limits); minima per
    category as the honest ancestor of DepthControl; go-around published in advance.
    → Teaches: candour under stakes is a format, not a tone — difficulty typeset in the same box
    as the promise, at the same size.

20. **IOF *International Specification for Sprint Orienteering Maps* (ISSprOM 2019-2)** —
    "features that are not important for navigation should not be mapped"; line weight encodes
    passability; deliberate symbol exaggeration with published rules; generalization doctrine
    (selective + graphical).
    → Steal: omission as discipline (an anti-list you can point at); encoding judgement into line
    weight rather than adjectives.
    → Teaches: restraint enforced by specification beats restraint by taste — laws can be this
    mechanical.

## Phase 1 v3 additions (researched for the vision integration, 2026-08-25)

21. **Hazel Markus & Paula Nurius, "Possible Selves" (*American Psychologist* 41(9), 1986,
    pp. 954–969)** — possible selves are "the cognitive components of hopes, fears, goals, and
    threats" and "function as incentives for future behavior"; they supply the evaluative context
    for the current self.
    → Steal: the landing shows a *specific, elaborated* larger self anchored to demonstrated work —
    not vague aspiration; "who becomes capable here" as concrete portraits of practice.
    → Teaches: the vision's "I want to become more" engine is a named, measurable construct —
    design for it by elaborating possible selves with evidence (paths shown), which is exactly
    what separates ignition from hustle marketing.

22. **Dacher Keltner & Jonathan Haidt, "Approaching awe…" (*Cognition & Emotion* 17(2), 2003);
    Paul Piff et al., "Awe, the Small Self, and Prosocial Behavior" (*JPSP* 108(6), 2015,
    pp. 883–899)** — awe arises from perceptual vastness + need for accommodation; across five
    studies (N=2,078) awe produced a "small self" which mediated increased generosity, ethical
    decision-making and decreased entitlement.
    → Steal: vast moments engineered to require accommodation (four territories at scale; horizon
    insets) while staying quiet in type — the small self does the moral work.
    → Teaches: the vision's ambition×humility paradox is one mechanism. Design the vast-honest
    moment once, correctly, instead of alternating hype and piety.

23. **Nielsen Norman Group, "Progressive Disclosure" (+ Carroll's training-wheels studies)** —
    deferring advanced material improves learnability, efficiency and error rate; benefits hold
    only with ≤2 disclosure levels per surface and strong information scent; users initially
    limited to core features later *outperformed* on advanced tasks.
    → Steal: L6's disclosure bounds — surface clean, one labelled step down, deep layers earned;
    every progression visibly scented.
    → Teaches: layered spirituality is not just ethically required, it is usability-optimal —
    newcomers learn better when the deepest layer is deferred but findable.

24. **NASA Graphics Standards Manual, NHB 1430.2 (Danne & Blackburn, 1975/76)** — the worm
    logotype: "unity, technological precision, thrust and orientation toward the future";
    Helvetica-based system; strict one/two-colour discipline with a single rationed NASA red.
    → Steal: civilization-scale feeling achieved through typographic reduction and one hot accent
    on disciplined grounds — the exact antidote to sci-fi cosplay (anti-list #14).
    → Teaches: "spacecraft" is a register of precision, not set dressing; this manual is the proof
    and the style ancestor for any direction answering the vision's interplanetary note.

25. **Central Consumer Protection Authority (India), Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of
    Dark Patterns, 2023** (under Consumer Protection Act 2019 §18; effective 30 Nov 2023) —
    Annexure 1 specifies "False Urgency" (false scarcity/time pressure) as a prohibited dark
    pattern; violations face CPA §89 penalties (imprisonment up to 2 years; fines to ₹10 lakh).
    → Steal: an external, citable anchor for anti-list #9 — our no-fake-scarcity stance is also
    the operating law for Indian e-commerce surfaces.
    → Teaches: ĀRK's honesty constraints coincide with regulation; compliance and brand voice
    point the same direction, so neither is ever traded away.
