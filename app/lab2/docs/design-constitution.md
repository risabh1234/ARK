### ★ The Atlas of Broken Maps — CHOSEN

Modern people cross four terrains daily — the measurable, the marketable, the wanted, the
inherited — carrying maps drawn by institutions that deny each other's borders. Nobody hands you
the whole map; you cope with three broken ones. ĀRK's work is cartographic confession: we admit
the maps disagree, and we draw the legend that lets you navigate by all four at once. This page is
a working atlas plate, not a panorama. It has a legend, because terrain without a key is
intimidation; it has coordinates, because every claim can be located; it has a stated scale,
because numbers without provenance are ornaments. Marginalia carries the sources; insets carry
the depth; fold lines give the eye places to rest between regions. The feeling sought is
orientation-as-relief — the specific gratitude of finally seeing a shape put over country you
already cross every day. Not awe; recognition. The unresolved areas are drawn as unmapped ground
is drawn on honest charts: marked, bounded, named, and returned to as survey continues. The
promise of the landing: you already know this landscape. Here is the first map that does not
pretend the four territories agree — and shows its work wherever they touch.

### 2 — The Honest Chart

A nautical chart is the most candid object design ever produced: every depth printed on it is
dated, every doubtful sounding is marked *doubtful*, and whole regions of open water are left
blank with a warning — not because the ocean there is empty, but because nobody has sounded it
yet. Modern charts even print their own biography: a small source diagram in the corner shows
which decade surveyed which patch of sea, lead-line or multibeam. This page borrows the
hydrographer's ethic. It treats knowledge as water: deep, navigable in parts, dangerous where
unsounded. Claims are soundings — point measurements paid for by someone's fieldwork. Where ĀRK
has read deeply, depths crowd the sheet; where a question is still open, the paper stays honest
white with a boundary and a name, never disguised as certainty. The feeling is the pilot's particular
trust: not faith in perfection, but confidence in an instrument that admits exactly where its
knowledge ends. You are a mariner entering unfamiliar water at night, and the most respectful thing
we can hand you is a chart that says, plainly: here be our soundings, here be our blanks, here is
when last we measured. Navigation does not require a complete map. It requires a true one.

### 3 — The Instrument Approach

Pilots trust approach plates because the plates assume failure. Every published procedure carries
three registers at once: a plan view of the track, a profile of the descent, and a table of
minima — and, before anyone needs it, the missed-approach procedure: exactly what to do when the
runway does not appear. Different aircraft categories get different minimum boxes, because
capability differs and the chart says so out loud. This page is built to that grammar. Its
sections are procedures, not pitches: what the claim is, how it descends, and what you may do if
it doesn't resolve — published in advance, in the same box, at the same size. Depth of reading is
a category choice, and the minima change accordingly, openly. The feeling aimed for is procedural
calm: the specific drop in heart rate when, breaking out of cloud, you find the next altitude and
the next action already printed in front of you. Nothing here depends on inspiration or good
weather. You are crew, in actual conditions, and the promise of the page is the profession's
oldest one: whatever happens on this approach, the go-around was designed before you departed.

### 4 — The Legible City

Ask residents to sketch their own city and Kevin Lynch's finding holds: what survives is not the
street grid but five elements — paths, edges, districts, nodes, landmarks — assembled into a
mental map that lets a stranger become a native. Cities that people can picture are cities people
can act in; Lynch gave this quality a name, imageability, and treated it as infrastructure. This
page aims to be sketched from memory. It organizes itself into districts a first-time visitor can
name afterwards; its paths are readable lines of travel; its edges are drawn where territories
actually change; its landmarks are few and therefore strong — one memorable object per region,
never a skyline of competing towers. Wayfinding happens without signage when form is legible, so
the page refuses signposts-as-decoration: if a label doesn't help you place yourself, it comes
off. The feeling sought is the resident's quiet pride: I know where I am here. The test of the
landing is brutally concrete — close the tab and draw the page. If you can reproduce its shape,
it worked; you now hold a mental map of what ĀRK does, and no claim was needed to install it.

### 5 — The Orienteering Course

Sprint-orienteering maps are governed by a fierce published rule: *features that are not important
for navigation should not be mapped.* Waste bins, hydrants, parking meters — omitted, however
real. Line weight means passability: thick black is uncrossable, thin is crossable, and you bet
your race on the difference. Symbols are deliberately exaggerated beyond true size so they remain
readable at speed, and the exaggeration rules are public. This page adopts the mapper's
contract. Everything printed earns its place by helping you decide; anything ornamental is off the
sheet, because at decision speed decoration is obstruction. Claims behave like controls on a
course: each one placed so you can verify it against the ground in front of you — the source one
tap away — and the course's difficulty is published honestly, because a map that flatters the
navigator gets them lost. The feeling is competition focus: alert, economical, slightly thrilled.
You are moving fast through a landscape of assertions, and the page's entire promise is this:
nothing here is mapped for beauty. Every mark is either your next control or it isn't on the map.

## Vision integration — v3 addendum (2026-08-25)

`ARK_Vision_Document.md` reframes what the atlas is *for*. Three integrations, ratified by Ojas:

**1. The cartographer's hand is shown, not erased.** Harley's finding was that map authority
historically flowed from *erased* authorship; ĀRK inverts this deliberately. The atlas now has a
declared fifth element beneath the four broken territories: the stated interpretive framework
(Vedānta / Kṛṣṇa consciousness) drawn as the cartographer's own visible hand — present on the
legend where relevant to interpretation, named at depth, never disguised as neutral ground.
This is not a softening of the voice law; it is its completion: an instrument company that hides
its calibration is doing exactly what the critical cartographers proved maps always do.

**2. The horizon scale is part of the legend.** Humanity → civilization → cosmos is the atlas's
framing sequence (vision §9). It renders as bounded, labelled space — an inset progression, never
a panoramic spectacle (anti-list #12 still applies).

**3. The emotional target widens from relief to ignition.** v1 sought orientation-as-relief
("recognition, not awe"). The vision adds the engine *"I don't want to waste my potential"* and
the paradox of extraordinary ambition grounded in humility ("I am not the center of existence").
Research shows this is one mechanism, not two tones: awe produces a "small self" which mediates
increased generosity, ethics and decreased entitlement (Piff et al., 2015, *JPSP* 108(6):883–899)
— so ambition and humility are co-emitted by the same designed moment when that moment involves
vastness plus accommodation (Keltner & Haidt, 2003). The landing must therefore emit **ignition
through honest scale**: the reader sees a larger possible self *and* a smaller ego in the same
breath, both anchored to shown work.

Layer discipline for the tradition enters as **L6** below, bounded by progressive-disclosure
usability research (NN/g: ≤2 disclosure levels per surface, strong information scent; Carroll's
training-wheels studies showed early-limited users later outperformed on advanced material).
Dignity-of-tone enforcement lives in anti-list items 13–14 and the contract hard limits — not in
a separate law.

## Laws (drafted for the Atlas — ratified individually)

- **L1 — The legend precedes the terrain.** Any device that encodes meaning (claim-type marks,
  strata colours, counters, icons) ships with a visible key reachable in the same viewport
  journey. A screenshot containing an unexplained encoding is a violation.
- **L2 — Every claim carries coordinates.** No assertion renders without adjacent provenance
  furniture: a source reference, a true count, a date, or an explicit Unresolved marker.
  Assertions floating free of locators are a violation.
- **L3 — Unmapped ground stays drawn.** Uncertainty renders as bounded, named space (Unresolved
  blocks, hatched regions, open-case lists) — never as blankness, omission, or hedging copy.
  Using silence to dodge is a violation.
- **L4 — Marginalia is load-bearing.** Provenance lives in dedicated margin/meta furniture, set
  in its own typographic role, visually distinct from reading text. It may be quiet; it may never
  be absent, merged into body copy, or decorative-only.
- **L5 — Scale is honest.** Numbers carry units and vintages; visual weight tracks evidential
  weight. The loudest element on a screen must be its best-supported claim — loud type on weak
  evidence is a violation.
- **L6 — Depth reveals the cartographer.** *(Ratified 2026-08-25.)* The interpretive framework
  appears progressively through the content layers, labelled wherever it appears — never pitched
  at the surface, never concealed in the depths. Disclosure respects usability limits (≤2 levels
  per surface, strong information scent). A screenshot showing the framework asserted in surface
  chrome is a violation; so is deep content using it without stratum/labelling.
